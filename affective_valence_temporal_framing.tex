\title{Affective Valence and Temporal Framing:\\
       A Computational Theory of Counterfactual Precision Redistribution}
\author{Manon Job, Ben White, Mahault Albarracin}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Emotions are temporally structured: regret concerns what has happened, anxiety concerns what will happen, and surprise concerns what is happening now. Within predictive processing, variational free energy (VFE) evaluates how well the agent's model fits past observations, while expected free energy (EFE) evaluates how well future observations will match preferences. We propose that this computational distinction provides a unified temporal taxonomy of affect: emotions inherit their temporal direction from whichever free energy quantity generates them. Building on this mapping, we formalise counterfactual temporal framing as the mechanism by which agents actively navigate the temporal structure of affect. Temporal framing is cast as policy selection within a partially observable Markov decision process, where each policy redistributes precision across temporal horizons via distinct transition matrices. We show that this redistribution emerges from EFE minimisation: high prediction error favours future-oriented framing (prospective counterfactual simulation), while stable beliefs with high positive-belief precision favour past-oriented framing (autobiographical narrative stabilisation). A three-channel valence decomposition, separating backward (model valence), present (reward valence), and forward (action valence) components, provides a direct readout of temporal orientation at each timestep. Simulations demonstrate that healthy agents balance temporal orientations adaptively, with past-oriented framing serving as a preparatory step for present engagement. Two extensions to the standard active inference framework capture affective pathology: \emph{asymmetric hedonic sensitivity}, where separate precision parameters for positive and negative outcomes model differential reward and punishment processing, and \emph{habit priors} (E~vectors), where additive log-priors over policies model habitual, EFE-suboptimal action tendencies. Chronic stress produces a pathological pattern: elevated future frame beliefs with depleted present engagement, persistent negative reward valence from frustrated forward projection, and a self-amplifying cycle where precise future predictions are systematically violated by a volatile environment. Temporal fixation (past-directed when habit priors override EFE-optimal escape, future-directed when amplified reward sensitivity overrides the energy brake) produces characteristic three-channel valence signatures that distinguish healthy metastability from pathological lock-in. We connect this framework to the mood--emotion timescale hierarchy, calibrate ten profiles in the Pleasure--Arousal--Dominance space, and situate it within the temporal aiming programme in active inference.
\end{abstract}




\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}


{\rr

Humans have the distinctive ability for mental time travel, insofar as they can simulate past, present, and future events to support decision-making. Moment-to-moment shifts in temporal orientation occur against a backdrop of more stable temporal dispositions, enabling individuals to balance long-term perspectives with the demands of everyday life. These shifts in temporal orientation are intertwined with specific emotions: regret is directed towards the past, surprise refocuses attention on the present, and hope is about the future.
A key question is how the past, present, and future are instrumentally recruited to guide behaviour towards preferred outcomes, and how emotions contribute to these processes.

We approach these questions within predictive processing, drawing on the strand of that tradition that treats emotional experience as an integral component of the inferential processes organising perception, learning, and action \citep{barrett2017theory, sethfriston2016, kiverstein2025}.}
On this view, affect is grounded in the agent's success at minimising prediction error, the discrepancy between expected and sensed input. It can be past oriented, as the derivative of the trajectory of an agent's model fit to its environment \citep{joffily2013emotional}: improved fit produces positive valence, deteriorating fit produces negative valence. This formulation grounds affect in the same variational free energy (VFE) quantity that drives perception and learning.

A complementary strand of work has shown that affect is also forward-looking. Expected free energy (EFE), the quantity minimised in active inference policy selection \citep{parr2022active}, evaluates how well future observations will match expectations  under each available policy. \citet{hesp2021deeply} showed that the ``affective charge'' of policy revision, that is, the degree to which the agent shifts probability toward better policies, constitutes a forward-looking valence signal. 
\citet{pattisapu2024free} extended the affective readout to include a present-oriented reward prediction error channel, completing a three-channel architecture spanning backward, present, and forward temporal orientations.

These contributions establish that affect has temporal structure. Yet no existing paper formally unifies the VFE/EFE distinction into a temporal taxonomy of emotion, nor provides a computational model of how organisms \emph{actively navigate} this temporal structure. \citet{hinrichs2025temporal} introduced ``temporal aiming'' in the context of geometric hyperscanning, showing how coupled active inference agents coordinate their affective temporal orientations during dyadic interaction. However, the single-agent computational mechanism, the process by which an individual agent selects which temporal horizon of free energy dominates its affective signal, remains unaddressed.

We address both gaps. We formally propose the VFE/EFE temporal mapping as a unified theory of affective temporal direction (\S\ref{sec:temporal_structure}), derive a three-channel valence architecture that decomposes affect into its temporal components (\S\ref{sec:decoupled}), and formalise counterfactual temporal framing as precision redistribution across temporal horizons (\S\ref{sec:counterfactual}). Temporal framing is not a separate cognitive function bolted onto inference; it \emph{emerges} from EFE minimisation once the generative model has the right structure. We introduce six temporal-framing policies: RECALL, ENGAGE, FUTURATE, FEEL, DISSOCIATE, and ABSTRACT. The first three implement the core past--present--future axis; the latter three model interoceptive regulation, temporal unmooring, and ungrounded abstraction. We also introduce two extensions to the standard active inference apparatus: \emph{asymmetric hedonic sensitivity} ($c_{\text{pos}} \neq c_{\text{neg}}$), which captures differential precision on positive versus negative hedonic outcomes, and \emph{habit priors} (E~vectors, following \citealt{dacosta2020active}), which model habitual, EFE-suboptimal action tendencies. We demonstrate the framework with simulations of past-oriented framing (\S\ref{sec:past}), future-oriented framing (\S\ref{sec:future}), the pathological case of chronic stress as maladaptive temporal fixation (\S\ref{sec:chronic_stress}), and a PAD-space calibration of ten emotion profiles (\S\ref{sec:pad_validation}). We close by outlining empirical validation routes using open ESM, reward-learning, and autobiographical-memory datasets (\S\ref{sec:empirical_validation}).

A companion paper applies this framework to bipolar disorder, demonstrating how the parameters give rise to depressive, manic, and psychotic phenotypes.


\section{Future thinking and resolution for engagement}\label{sec:future_thinking}

\subsection{Mental time travel and the problem of scope}\label{sec:timetravel}
For much of everyday cognition, agents mentalise goals and the paths towards them, detaching from the immediate present to simulate the future. These simulated futures are built out of memories and past learning, so much so that remembering and future thinking have been argued to rely on a single machinery. These simulations must be precise enough to guide action, yet they are built with finite resources, a constraint that becomes especially apparent in humans, as we juggle timescales ranging from the immediate to the lifelong. Prima facie, planning over a longer horizon requires more state-to-state transitions than planning over a shorter one. But transitions are not free. Tree search over policies grows with both the branching factor and the depth of search, so exhaustive planning is intractable for any system with bounded resources. Nor do transitions accumulate without cost: each rollout step introduces approximation error, and these errors compound along the projected trajectory.

The empirical literature on future-oriented cognition shows how humans handle this problem \citep{schacteraddisbuckner2007, benedeketal2023}. At very long temporal horizons, little fine-grained arbitration takes place at all: agents rely instead on broad, optimistic beliefs that preserve orientation without detailed simulation. A young person may picture themselves as a contented grandparent in retirement and take concrete steps toward that image, for example having children, securing a good pension, without attempting to compute everything that might go wrong along the way. 


Rather than constructing every future scenario at a fixed resolution, with equal temporal intervals between successive states, agents ``zoom'' in and out across temporal scales. Semantic and conceptual knowledge links the present context both to memories of past events and to anticipated future goals, and the number and resolution of the intermediary steps computed along the way vary with environmental complexity, novelty, uncertainty, affective load, and other task-relevant constraints \citep{dargembeauvanderlinden2004, benedeketal2023}. Stress is a case in point: it can narrow the agent's temporal horizon, biasing cognition toward immediate affordances and reducing the availability of distant goals.

\subsection{The role of optimism}\label{sec:optimism}

These variables track scope itself. Near-past and near-future events tend to be represented concretely and in fine-grained detail, whereas distant past and future events are represented in more abstract and schematic terms \citep{tropeliberman2010, lacortepiolino2016}. 

Entertaining such rosy beliefs is tied to the mechanisms by which agents aim at the future when detailed planning is intractable. This is computationally sensible, since the optimal path to happiness fifty years hence is intractable to specify; and when agents do fill in distant detail, the exercise tends to provoke anxiety rather than a sense of mastery, presumably because such detail offers little practical guidance at that temporal distance \citep{tropeliberman2010, sharot2011}.

 On Fredrickson's broaden-and-build theory, positive emotions widen the scope of available thoughts and actions, orienting the agent toward more exploratory and flexible behavioural routines that may, over time, consolidate into new habits and repeatable practices \citep{fredrickson2001, fredrickson2004, fredrickson2013, lenguyenfredrickson2017}. \citet{fredrickson2004} argues, in addition, that positive emotions are less action-specific than negative ones: negative affect often demands an immediate and determinate response, as in fear before an attack, whereas positive affect leaves the field of possible action open \citep{gableharmonjones2010}. Future aiming is thus supported by beliefs that need not specify the future in detail, but only orient the agent toward a viable direction of travel \citep{fisherhohwy2024, fisheretal2025}.

\subsection{Zoom scale and the modulation of goal-directed control}\label{sec:zoom}
The variable resolution just described bears on a long-standing question in the theory of action. That question is distinct from the division between goal-directed and habitual control, but it interacts with recent work that treats the space between the two poles as a continuum rather than a switch \citep{pezzulorigolichersi2013, fitzgeralddolanfriston2014, dolandayan2013}. On the standard schema, goal-directed control is deliberative and model-based: the agent simulates the consequences of candidate actions before choosing, drawing on abstract knowledge, on episodic memories of what happened where and when, and on the structure of the task at hand. Habitual control is its slower-forming, model-free counterpart: actions that have proved reliable are compressed into cue-triggered responses, a cache that approximates the outcome of deliberation without recomputing it \citep{fitzgeralddolanfriston2014, maistoetal2019}.

Our account is consistent with these graded, intermediate treatments, but recasts the continuum in temporal terms. We will see that a past-oriented mode leans on regularities slowly distilled from experience, whereas a future-oriented mode is less tied to goal attainment in the current context.

\subsection{Two heuristics and the identity pole}\label{sec:two_faces}
To develop this idea, we draw on the literature on memory retrieval and future thinking as it bears on decision making. The depth at which a plan is unrolled, and with it the agent's capacity to sustain optimism, is bound up with identity and self-narrative. Decision theory distinguishes two families of action-selection heuristics. The first works from identity. On the logic of appropriateness, the agent asks what kind of situation this is, what kind of person they are, and what such a person does here. Choosing means recognising a situation and completing a pattern from a self-representation, not computing outcomes \citep{marcholsen1989, march1994, weberetal2004}. The second works from the task. On the logic of consequences, the agent lays out candidate action--outcome sequences and scores them against the goal. The distinction has been given computational form, with appropriateness-based choice modelled as predictive pattern completion over culturally acquired knowledge, and consequentialist choice as rollout \citep{leiboetal2024}.\footnote{The philosophical lineage of the first family runs through the notion of \emph{practical identity} as a source of reasons for action \citep{korsgaard1996} and narrative self-constitution \citep{schechtman1996}.} The two logics differ in how deeply the plan is unrolled. Appropriateness conditions choice on slow, temporally extended beliefs about who one is, and stays shallow; it is the angler's winter plan. Consequence-based control descends toward episode-level simulation; it is the plan drawn up at the water's edge.

The identity pole is also a broad motif in psychology and economics. People act more readily, and read difficulty differently, when the action fits a currently salient identity \citep{oyserman2009}, and making a different identity salient shifts choice \citep{leboeufetal2010}. Formal models split the value of an act into an outcome component and a diagnostic component, the second capturing what the choice would say about the kind of person one is \citep{akerlofkranton2000, bodnerprelec2003}. Models of self-regulation treat identity as an input to valuation rather than a constraint imposed from outside \citep{berkmanetal2017}, and habits are most stable when tied to self-definition \citep{verplankensui2019}. Under the standard partition of control into model-based and model-free systems \citep{dawetal2005, dolandayan2013}, these identity processes would land on the model-free side. We think this filing misses what the literature on identity, memory, and future thinking shows. On our account, identity-congruent action is not the read-out of cached action values. It is evaluation against a high-level prior: choice conditioned on the agent's slowest and most abstract self-representations, run without deep unrolling. The heuristic is shallow in temporal depth, not empty of goals or blind to cues.

\subsection{Retrieval constraints, interoception, and overgeneral memory}\label{sec:retrieval_constraints}
The memory literature makes these identity constraints a mechanism, in branches that computational work has not fully absorbed. In the Self-Memory System, retrieval is a negotiation between adaptive correspondence, fidelity to experience-near sensory records in the service of current goals, and self-coherence, the pressure to keep what is remembered consistent with the conceptual self. The working self gates construction as retrieval moves down the autobiographical hierarchy \citep{conwaypleydellpearce2000, conwaysingertagini2004, conway2005}. A retrieval that stops high in the hierarchy, at a self-congruent generalisation, is the identity heuristic applied to the past. One that reaches a specific episode pays for correspondence. And whether the working self can reach a specific, self-congruent episode at all depends on the agent's current affective and interoceptive state and on its emotional granularity: coarse, negatively valenced states narrow access to specific, positively toned material \citep{bower1981, forgas1995, sethfriston2016, pezzulorigolifriston2015}.

The failure mode of the shallow setting has its own literature. The overgeneral retrieval catalogued by the CaR-FA-X programme, which grew out of the Self-Memory System, is what it looks like when coherence wins over correspondence: the cue is answered from the kind of person one takes oneself to be, not from an episode \citep{williamsetal2007, watkinsteasdale2001}. Overgeneral memory is, in this sense, the clinical signature of the identity heuristic applied to the past, and it is the retrospective truncation whose computational form we recover in \S\ref{sec:feedback_reliance}.

\subsection{Past and future orientedness: one depth regulator}\label{sec:one_regulator}
This paper thus offers a computational account of graded, model-based goal-directed control in which the depth of simulation is a regulated quantity. How far a memory or a plan is unrolled depends on environmental constraints, learning history, and stress, and the arbitration weighs identity fit alongside computational cost. The account draws on the psychological literature on memory retrieval and future thinking, and on its correlates in interoception, emotional granularity, and the constraints on getting specific (\S\ref{sec:retrieval_constraints}).

The regulator has two temporal faces. On the past side, the agent arbitrates between self-coherence and correspondence. Its healthy shallow setting is the positively biased working self: the optimism heuristic is available only so long as affect is positive enough to sustain a coherent, self-congruent prior. When affect turns negative and construal stays coarse, an agent stuck at this setting produces the overgeneral retrieval of \S\ref{sec:retrieval_constraints}. On the future side, the healthy shallow setting is orientation without detailed simulation; a plan is committed to only once it has been computed and broken into steps, and the depth of that computation is bounded by its cost.

The two temporal directions of failure map onto the classical split between rumination, past-directed and self-focused, and worry, future-directed and anticipatory \citep{fresco2002, nolenhoeksema2008}. Rumination arises when a habitual, identity-congruent bias overrides the escape that consequence-based control would favour: the shallow route can no longer deliver optimism, yet the agent keeps answering from it. Worry arises when a bias toward reward overrides the cost that normally limits forward simulation: the deep route is recruited and does not stop. This double bind is the computational core of the chronic-stress pathology of \S\ref{sec:chronic_stress}. The next section gives the depth regulator a formal treatment in the language of free energy.

\subsection{VFE as Backward-Looking Evaluation}\label{sec:vfe}





Variational free energy is the backward-looking evaluation in active inference, {\rr asking ``did I predict well?''. Based on sensed errors, the VFE value increases or decreases, grounding how the organism then balances perception and learning for better attunement with its environment.} For a generative model $p(o, s)$ with approximate posterior $q(s)$ over hidden states $s$ given observations $o$:
\begin{equation}
    F = \underbrace{-\mathbb{E}_{q(s)}[\ln p(o \mid s)]}_{\text{accuracy}} + \underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}[q(s) \,\|\, p(s)]}_{\text{complexity}}
\end{equation}
$F$ evaluates how well the \emph{current} model fits \emph{past} observations: accuracy measures the expected log-likelihood of what has already been observed, while complexity penalises deviation from prior beliefs. 

Minimising $F$  with respect to the approximate posterior, $q(s)$, corresponds to perception: the agent updates its current estimate of hidden states, $s$, in light of incoming observations. Minimising $VFE$ with respect to the generative model, $p(o,s)$, corresponds to learning.
{\rr Broadly speaking, this means that perception adjusts the agent’s current inference about the hidden causes of sensory input, whereas learning recalibrates the parameters of the generative model over time, on the basis of accumulated prediction error, so as to better capture the longer-term statistical regularities of the environment.
}


\citet{joffily2013emotional} showed that the temporal derivative of $F$ provides a natural valence signal:
\begin{equation}
    -\frac{dF}{dt}: \quad \text{rate of model improvement}
\end{equation}
Decreasing $F$ (improving model fit) produces positive valence; increasing $F$ produces negative valence. The quantity is backward-looking: it evaluates the \emph{trajectory} of model fit over recent observations.

The second temporal derivative $-d^2F/dt^2$ further differentiates affective states by their dynamics. When both $dF$ and $d^2F$ are negative, the model fit is improving at an accelerating rate, corresponding to \textbf{hope}. When $dF$ is negative but $d^2F$ is positive, improvement is decelerating and stabilising into a good state, corresponding to \textbf{happiness}. Conversely, when both are positive, model fit is worsening at an accelerating rate, corresponding to \textbf{fear}, while negative $d^2F$ with positive $dF$ indicates stabilisation into a bad state, corresponding to \textbf{unhappiness}. Sign changes in $dF$ mark affective transitions: a shift from positive to negative $dF$ corresponds to \textbf{relief}, while a shift from negative to positive corresponds to \textbf{disappointment}. This taxonomy derives entirely from the dynamics of a single backward-looking quantity.

\end{equation}
where the sum ranges over observation modalities $m$, $q(o_m \mid \pi)$ is the predicted observation distribution under policy $\pi$, and $p(o_m \mid C_m)$ encodes preferences. \section{A metacognitive approach to VFE and EFE quantities}



\subsection{General introduction to the model}\label{sec:decoupled}

{\rr


We model the agent's internal architecture using a factored partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), where the hidden state $s = (v, e, f)$ is decomposed into three latent dimensions: valence ($v$), representing affective state at granularity $K$; energy ($e$), reflecting the sensed allostatic resource estimate; and temporal frame ($f$), denoting the agent's active counterfactual orientation. This architecture supports three capacities: interoceptive monitoring of allostatic demand, transitions between abstract valence states, and shifts of cognitive focus across past, present, and future horizons. The temporal frame is a latent state inferred by Bayesian updating. The posterior belief $q(f \mid o)$, derived by marginalizing over valence and energy, gives a direct readout of the agent's current temporal orientation. Attending to a temporal frame redistributes precision over the corresponding latent states and changes the EFE composition of available policies. In this sense, temporal framing is inferred through EFE minimization.


The distribution of attention across these states requires second-order inference, through which the agent estimates which free-energy quantity, past, present, or future, should be prioritised in order to minimise EFE. This metacognitive evaluation is implemented through distinct valence-channel readings, corresponding to successive stages in the agent--environment loop:
\[
\text{observation}
\rightarrow
\underbrace{\text{belief update}}_{v_{\text{model}}}
\rightarrow
\underbrace{\text{EFE recomputation}}_{v_{\text{reward}}}
\rightarrow
\underbrace{\text{policy update}}_{v_{\text{action}}}.
\]


$v_{\text{model}}$ evaluates how an observation alters the agent's posterior beliefs about world states; 
$v_{\text{reward}}$ evaluates how those updated beliefs alter expected future outcomes, as indexed by EFE; 
and $v_{\text{action}}$ evaluates how revised expectations constrain policy selection.

In this architecture, the metacognitive system does not merely react to free energy. Rather, it actively evaluates which temporal form of free energy should become the dominant target of precision-weighted attention.}



\subsection{Channel 1: Model Valence $v_{\text{model}}$ (Backward)}\label{sec:v_model}

Following \citet{joffily2013emotional}, model valence tracks the rate of change of variational free energy:
\begin{equation}
    v_{\text{model}} = \tanh\!\left(\frac{-\Delta F}{\tau_{\text{model}}}\right)
\end{equation}
where $\Delta F = F(t) - F(t{-}1)$. Decreasing free energy (improving model fit) produces positive valence; increasing free energy produces negative valence.

Functionally, this channel modulates the \emph{model} learning rate (distinct from the arousal-driven \emph{policy} precision discussed in \S\ref{sec:loop}). Persistently negative $v_{\text{model}}$ signals that the generative model is deteriorating, which should accelerate parameter updating ($\sigma_{\text{eff}} = \sigma \times \exp(-\alpha \cdot v_{\text{model}})$). Its temporal character is strictly backward: it evaluates the current state relative to the model's past predictions.

\subsection{Channel 2: Reward Valence $v_{\text{reward}}$ (Present)}\label{sec:v_reward}

Following \citet{pattisapu2024free}, reward valence captures the momentary reward prediction error:
\begin{equation}
    v_{\text{reward}} = \tanh\!\left(\frac{U - \mathbb{E}[U]}{\tau_{\text{reward}}}\right)
\end{equation}
where $U = \sum_{m \in \mathcal{H}} \log P(o_m \mid C_m)$ is the utility of the observed outcome and $\mathbb{E}[U] = \sum_{m \in \mathcal{H}} \sum_o Q(o_m) \log P(o_m \mid C_m)$ is the expected utility under predicted observations, restricted to the \emph{hedonic} modalities $\mathcal{H} = \{o_{\text{ext}}, o_{\text{val}}\}$. The interoceptive modality is excluded from felt valence because body-budget maintenance is largely preconscious \citep{seth2016interoceptive, barrett2017theory}: interoceptive prediction errors drive allostatic regulation (via the EFE policy selection) but do not produce conscious hedonic experience.

This channel drives preference monitoring: chronic negative $v_{\text{reward}}$ signals that the agent's preferences ($\mathbf{C}$ vectors) may be unrealistic. Its temporal character is present-oriented: it evaluates \emph{this} observation relative to what was expected.

\subsection{Channel 3: Action Valence $v_{\text{action}}$ (Forward)}\label{sec:v_action}

Following \citet{hesp2021deeply}, action valence captures the affective charge of policy revision:
\begin{equation}
    v_{\text{action}} = \tanh\!\left(\frac{\text{AC}}{\tau_{\text{action}}}\right), \qquad \text{AC} = (\boldsymbol{\pi}_{t-1} - \boldsymbol{\pi}_t) \cdot \mathbf{G}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\pi}$ is the policy distribution and $\mathbf{G}$ the expected free energy vector. Positive affective charge indicates the agent is shifting probability \emph{away from} high-EFE policies (planning improvement); negative charge indicates deterioration.

This channel drives precision updating: positive $v_{\text{action}}$ increases the effective inverse temperature $\bar{\beta}$ (more decisive policy selection), while negative $v_{\text{action}}$ decreases it (more exploratory). Its temporal character is forward-looking: it evaluates whether the agent's \emph{planning} is improving.

\subsection{Composite Valence and Channel Disagreement}\label{sec:composite}

The three channels combine into a single bounded signal:
\begin{equation}
    V = \tanh(v_{\text{model}} + v_{\text{reward}} + v_{\text{action}})
\end{equation}

When channels agree, the affective signal is unambiguous: genuine satisfaction when all are positive, or genuine distress when all are negative. When they disagree, the resulting states are informationally rich. A state of \emph{productive failure} arises when $v_{\text{model}} < 0$ and $v_{\text{action}} > 0$: the current observation was bad, but the agent found a better plan, corresponding to learning from mistakes. \emph{Unsettling success} occurs when $v_{\text{reward}} > 0$ but $v_{\text{action}} < 0$: the outcome was good, but the planning landscape worsened, suggesting that good fortune may be unsustainable. \emph{Dawning anxiety} appears when $v_{\text{model}} \approx 0$, $v_{\text{reward}} \approx 0$, and $v_{\text{action}} < 0$: nothing has happened, but plans are deteriorating, producing the computational signature of anticipatory dread.

\subsection{Temporal Orientation as Normalised Channel Contribution}\label{sec:temporal_orientation}

We define the temporal orientation at each timestep as the normalised absolute channel contribution:
\begin{equation}
    \phi_{\text{dir}}(t) = \frac{|v_{\text{dir}}(t)|}{|v_{\text{model}}(t)| + |v_{\text{reward}}(t)| + |v_{\text{action}}(t)| + \epsilon}
\end{equation}
and the \emph{backward--forward conflict rate} as the fraction of timesteps where $v_{\text{model}}$ and $v_{\text{action}}$ have opposite signs.

Different emotions have characteristic temporal orientation profiles. Rumination is backward-dominated with high conflict; impulsivity is present-dominated with low conflict; anxiety is forward-dominated with high conflict. This connects the three-channel architecture back to the VFE/EFE temporal mapping of \S\ref{sec:temporal_mapping}: the channels provide a continuous, per-timestep readout of which temporal horizon currently dominates the agent's affect.



\subsection{Temporal Framing and the Emotion--Action--Inference Loop}\label{sec:loop}


In summary, the integrative cycle linking temporal framing to affect is represented as follows: \(\mathrm{EFE} \to \mathrm{policy} \to \mathrm{action} \to \mathrm{observation} \to \mathrm{VFE} \to v_{\mathrm{action}} \to \mathrm{precision} \to \mathrm{EFE}\).

The pivotal feedback mechanism is the forward valence channel, \(v_{\mathrm{action}}\). A positive affective charge, denoting policy improvement, elevates the effective inverse temperature, \(\bar{\gamma}\), thereby refining subsequent policy selection \(\left(\S\ref{sec:v_action}\right)\), whereas a negative affective charge encourages policy exploration. The backward and present channels, \(v_{\mathrm{model}}\) and \(v_{\mathrm{reward}}\), function as affective readouts of the loop's dynamics rather than constitutive causal links. Although these readouts lack a direct causal role within the internal loop. Arousal, conceptualized as posterior state entropy \(H[q(s \mid o)]\) following \citet{pattisapu2024free}, exists orthogonally to valence within the circumplex model: it tracks the agent's uncertainty regarding current hidden states, independent of the direction of free energy flux.

\section{Counterfactual Temporal Framing as Precision Redistribution}\label{sec:counterfactual}

Adaptive flexibility involves navigating this cognitive architecture rather than remaining constrained to a single temporal horizon. In self-regulation, the loop maintains homeostasis: elevated VFE initiates corrective action, reducing prediction error; the resulting positive \(v_{\mathrm{action}}\) sharpens precision, reinforcing the corrective trajectory \citep{joffily2013emotional}. In self-amplification, the loop becomes pathological: temporal framing becomes fixed within a single mode, precision on that mode's prediction errors intensifies, and the agent becomes trapped within the attractor. This transition from self-regulation to self-amplification constitutes the computational basis of chronic stress \(\left(\S\ref{sec:chronic_stress}\right)\).

This transition between temporal modes means the recruitment of distinct actions, including the aforementioned \textsc{Recall} and \textsc{Futurate} strategies. Each action defines a unique transition structure, \(\mathbf{B}_a = \mathbf{B}_v^{(a)} \otimes \mathbf{B}_e^{(a)} \otimes \mathbf{B}_f^{(a)}\). In this sense, the frame transition matrices, \(\mathbf{B}_f^{(a)}\), function as temporal precision operators, redistributing precision across temporal frames and thereby determining the horizon to which the agent attends.

\paragraph{RECALL.} The frame matrix $\mathbf{B}_f^{\textsc{recall}}$ assigns 70\% self-transition to \textsc{past}, allocating precision to past-generated internal states. Simultaneously, $\mathbf{B}_v^{\textsc{recall}}$ pulls the valence state toward a \emph{bidirectional} target with strength $\alpha = \sigma(\pi_{\text{pos}} - \theta)$. The target itself depends on $\alpha$: $v_{\text{recall}} = 0.2 + 0.6\alpha$, so high $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ pulls toward positive past ($\approx 80\%$ of max valence, narrative stabilisation) while low $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ pulls toward negative past ($\approx 28\%$ of max valence, rumination).

\paragraph{ENGAGE.} The frame matrix $\mathbf{B}_f^{\textsc{engage}}$ assigns 75\% self-transition to \textsc{present}, maintaining precision on current sensory input.

\paragraph{FUTURATE.} The frame matrix $\mathbf{B}_f^{\textsc{futurate}}$ assigns 90\% self-transition to \textsc{future}, allocating precision to anticipated future states. The high absorptivity reflects ``narrative inertia'': once engaged in prospective simulation, the agent tends to persist. Simultaneously, $\mathbf{B}_v^{\textsc{futurate}}$ pulls valence toward the solution state $K{-}1$, imagining the best outcome.

\paragraph{FEEL (active interoceptive processing).} The frame matrix $\mathbf{B}_f^{\textsc{feel}}$ assigns 50--60\% to \textsc{present}, releasing frame commitment and allowing the temporal orientation to reset. FEEL models active interoceptive processing: the reduction of accumulated prediction error through body-state monitoring \citep{stephan2016allostatic}, with saturating returns as load approaches the homeostatic setpoint.

\paragraph{DISSOCIATE (dissociative null action).} The frame matrix $\mathbf{B}_f^{\textsc{dissociate}}$ distributes probability roughly uniformly across temporal frames ($\approx 0.35$ each), modelling \emph{temporal unmooring}: disengagement from directed temporal processing without anchoring to any frame. This is consistent with the finding that dissociation is positively associated with undirected past and future thinking, inversely with present-moment awareness \citep{vannikov2018dissociation}.

\paragraph{ABSTRACT (effortful ungrounded cognition).} The frame matrix $\mathbf{B}_f^{\textsc{abstract}}$ assigns $\approx 65$--$80\%$ to \textsc{future}, coupling with FUTURATE to form a second hedonic route that bypasses interoception and episodic recall. ABSTRACT is therefore future-pulling without the body-budget constraint supplied by FEEL or the autobiographical constraint supplied by RECALL.

\paragraph{Learned frame transitions.} The $\mathbf{B}_{\text{frame}}$ matrices are initialised with the values described above but are updated online via Dirichlet learning. Each action's frame transition matrix is parameterised by Dirichlet pseudo-counts $\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(a)}$, initialised at $\mathbf{B}_f^{(a)} \cdot \kappa$ where $\kappa = 50$ sets the initial concentration. After each transition, the counts update using the soft frame posteriors: $\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(a)}_{f', f} \mathrel{+}= q(f' \mid o_t) \cdot q(f \mid o_{t-1})$. The expected transition matrix $\bar{\mathbf{B}}_f^{(a)} = \boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(a)} / \sum_{f'} \boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(a)}_{f'}$ then replaces the fixed matrix. This allows the agent to learn personalised temporal attention operators: an agent that frequently and successfully uses RECALL will strengthen its PAST self-transition, while an agent that rarely uses FUTURATE will allow its FUTURE transition to decay toward the prior.

\paragraph{Preserved interoceptive preferences.} The preference vectors $\mathbf{C}_m$ are scaled by reward sensitivity for the hedonic modalities ($\mathbf{C}_{\text{ext}}$, $\mathbf{C}_{\text{val}}$), but the interoceptive preference $\mathbf{C}_{\text{int}}$ is \emph{not} scaled. Interoceptive preferences (the body-budgeting system that monitors metabolic state) remain intact even when hedonic sensitivity is low. This dissociation is supported by evidence that effort-cost computation is preserved under reduced reward sensitivity \citep{treadway2012effort} and that allostatic self-efficacy operates as a separate system from hedonic reward \citep{stephan2016allostatic}.

\paragraph{Asymmetric hedonic sensitivity.} We extend the scalar reward sensitivity parameter $c_{\text{scale}}$ to a pair $(c_{\text{pos}}, c_{\text{neg}})$ that separately scale the positive and negative entries of the hedonic preference vectors:
\begin{equation}
    C_m(o) = \begin{cases}
        c_{\text{pos}} \cdot C_m^{\text{raw}}(o) & \text{if } C_m^{\text{raw}}(o) > 0 \\
        c_{\text{neg}} \cdot C_m^{\text{raw}}(o) & \text{if } C_m^{\text{raw}}(o) < 0 \\
        0 & \text{if } C_m^{\text{raw}}(o) = 0
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
for $m \in \{\text{ext}, \text{val}\}$, where $C_m^{\text{raw}}$ denotes the baseline preference vector. When $c_{\text{pos}} = c_{\text{neg}} = c_{\text{scale}}$, this reduces to symmetric scaling. The asymmetric parameterisation captures differential precision on positive versus negative hedonic outcomes. In the EFE decomposition, low $c_{\text{pos}}$ flattens the risk reduction achievable by moving toward preferred states, while high $c_{\text{neg}}$ amplifies the risk associated with negative outcomes. This asymmetry is mechanistically distinct from overall reward suppression ($c_{\text{scale}} \to 0$): an agent with $c_{\text{pos}} \ll c_{\text{neg}}$ retains strong aversion to negative states while losing the capacity for positive hedonic experience. The asymmetric parameterisation is empirically motivated by findings that reward sensitivity and punishment sensitivity dissociate in mood disorders \citep{eshel2010reward, pizzagalli2014depression}.

\paragraph{Habit priors (E~vector).} Standard active inference selects policies purely via EFE minimisation: $\pi(a) \propto \exp(-\gamma \cdot G(a))$. \citet{dacosta2020active} introduced an additive log-prior $\mathbf{E}$ over policies, the ``E~vector'' or habit prior, which encodes habitual or automatic tendencies independent of current EFE:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:e_vector}
    \ln \pi(a) = -\gamma \cdot G(a) + E_a + \text{const}
\end{equation}
When $\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{0}$, policy selection reduces to EFE minimisation. Non-zero entries bias policy selection toward (positive $E_a$) or away from (negative $E_a$) specific actions. The E~vector provides the formal mechanism for modelling \emph{maladaptive habits}: actions that are suboptimal under EFE but are nevertheless selected due to ingrained habitual tendencies.

In the temporal framing domain, the E~vector provides a natural account of maladaptive temporal fixation. An agent with $E_{\textsc{recall}} \gg 0$ and $E_{\textsc{futurate}} < 0$ is biased toward past-directed processing regardless of whether RECALL is EFE-optimal, while $E_{\textsc{futurate}} \gg 0$ would bias toward forward projection. This captures the general phenomenon of habitual temporal orientation: the agent is drawn to a particular framing mode not because it reduces expected free energy, but because it has become a default response to distress \citep{watkins2008constructive, ehring2008repetitive}. A companion paper develops this mechanism in the context of clinical rumination.


\subsection{Navigating the temporal precision operators}



When the agent selects RECALL, $\mathbf{B}_v^{\textsc{recall}}$ pulls beliefs toward a valence target that depends on the agent's positive-belief precision: agents with high $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ recall positive past ($\approx 80\%$ of max), while agents with low $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ recall negative past ($\approx 28\%$ of max). In both cases, the agent is re-accessing its past self-model, running inference on what its affective state \emph{was} (or what it \emph{could have been} had things gone differently).
Moreover, the \emph{content} of that recall is shaped by the precision of positive self-beliefs introducing a positive-negative axis, which is to be understood in its commonsensical meaning ("being dirty is bad, clean is good"). While this quantity is explicitly encoded and distinct from valenced variations of precision, it could arguably be reduced to a single quantity of nested message passing in a more sophisticated model ,  we argue in favour of this view in Section XX.
When the agent selects FUTURATE, $\mathbf{B}_v^{\textsc{futurate}}$ pulls beliefs toward the solution state: the agent is imagining its best possible future, running inference on what its affective state \emph{will be} under the most optimistic scenario.



\section{Past-Oriented Framing: Narrative Stabilisation}\label{sec:past}

{\rr RECALL is the computational realisation of \emph{shallow} model based, goal directedness \S\ref{sec:one_regulator}. Where \S\ref{sec:two_faces} characterised the identity heuristic as recognising a situation and completing a pattern from a self-representation rather than computing outcomes, RECALL implements exactly this: a low-cost policy that answers the current situation from a self-congruent prior, pulling belief toward the conceptual self rather than unrolling consequences. In health, and in line with the positively biased working self of \S\ref{sec:retrieval_constraints} \citep{berntsen2009, conwaypleydellpearce2000, conway2005}, that prior is optimistic: retrieval is cue-triggered, cheap, and lands on a positive past. The present model thereby suggests that a single mechanism reproduces several key features of the literatures on autobiographical memory, optimism, and schema use.}

\subsection{Mechanism of RECALL}\label{sec:recall_mechanism}

In the model, the RECALL policy operates through two mechanisms, both moderated by the precision of positive recall. First, when an agent can generate highly precise positive beliefs, RECALL increases the probability of attending to these representations by shifting frame beliefs toward the past. This is implemented as $\mathbf{B}_f^{\textsc{recall}}$ shifts frame beliefs toward \textsc{past} (70\% self-transition). Second, in parallel, $\mathbf{B}_v^{\textsc{recall}}$ pulls the valence state toward a \emph{bidirectional target} whose location depends on the same positive-belief precision that gates pull strength:
\begin{equation}
    v_{\text{recall}} = 0.2 + 0.6\,\alpha, \qquad \alpha = \sigma(\pi_{\text{pos}} - \theta)
\end{equation}
The pull target is given by $(K{-}1) \times v_{\text{recall}}$, while $\alpha$ determines the strength of the transition. Thus, both the direction and magnitude of recall are regulated by $\pi_{\text{pos}}$.

{\rr Positivity here is explicitly encoded rather than an emergent value of precision fluctuation. In its most immediate sense it is the subjective valence attributed to a recalled belief or autobiographical representation, the degree to which it is experienced as desirable and self-congruent, that is, as material the working self can assimilate without paying a coherence cost.


Thus, read through the Self-Memory System of \S\ref{sec:retrieval_constraints}, $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ is the precision of the conceptual self, and the gating $\alpha = \sigma(\pi_{\text{pos}} - \theta)$ is the working self gating construction as retrieval descends the autobiographical hierarchy \citep{conwaypleydellpearce2000, conway2005}. The bidirectional pull then formalises the negotiation between \emph{self-coherence} and \emph{correspondence} \citep{conwaysingertagini2004}: when $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ is high, coherence pressure and goal-relevance align, and the search is routed toward self-congruent, positively valenced material, the self-enhancing bias of the healthy adult; when $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ is low, the prior has too little precision to organise the descent, the pull is weak, and where it lands it is negative, the entry point to rumination. 
}


The sigmoid gating function produces an all-or-nothing effect at the threshold $\pi_{\text{pos}} \approx 2.0$. When $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ is high ($\alpha \to 1$), RECALL effectively generates positive counterfactuals grounded in a confident self-model, pulling toward $\approx 80\%$ of max valence. When $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ is low ($\alpha \approx 0.14$), RECALL is both impaired and \emph{ruminative}: the weak pull is directed toward $\approx 28\%$ of max valence (negative past), consistent with the ``overgeneral autobiographical memory'' phenomenon \citep{williams2007autobiographical}. % RESTORED: "impaired and ruminative" framing + OGM citation at this point
At intermediate $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ (e.g., chronic stress, $\pi_{\text{pos}} = 2.5$, $\alpha \approx 0.62$), the recall target is $\approx 57\%$ of max valence, still positive-leaning but less extreme than healthy, capturing the attenuated but not yet pathological recall of stress-exposed individuals. % RESTORED: intermediate-case analysis was missing entirely

Finally, RECALL carries a moderate energetic cost, specified as $-0.3$ per step. Episodic reconstruction is effortful, but less costly than FUTURATE, whose cost is set at $-0.5$ per step. RECALL is not treated as a cached reflex. It is a shallow inference against a high-precision self-prior: goal-directed, representationally structured, but limited in temporal depth. This gives RECALL a natural advantage in the EFE decomposition: substantial valence risk reduction at modest energy cost. Its breakdown should therefore look like impoverished retrieval, not simply the loss of a habit.

\footnote{For a comprehensive account of episodic encoding and retrieval in the context of deep temporal active inference, see Parr et al. (2026).}

\subsection{RECALL Supports Engagement}\label{sec:recall_engagement}

Past-oriented framing, we argue, is \emph{preparatory}: narrative consolidation enables action rather than substituting for it. The RECALL$\to$ENGAGE pipeline operates as follows: RECALL stabilises beliefs by pulling toward the prior, which lowers VFE. Computationally, this is indistinguishable from confirmatory perception: the agent pulls its own beliefs toward confirmation through mental simulation of goal attainment. {\rr This is the optimism heuristic of \S\ref{sec:optimism} in action: a coarse, positively biased prior preference that need not specify the future in full, only point the agent in a viable direction, so that instrumental optimism supports coarse but useful goal selection \citep{fisherhohwy2024, tschantzetal2020}.}

Lower VFE means the ENGAGE policy becomes more competitive (its risk terms decrease when beliefs are closer to preferred states). The agent then transitions from RECALL to ENGAGE as its beliefs stabilise. {\rr The pipeline thereby gives mechanistic form to the identity-based motivation findings of \S\ref{sec:two_faces}: people act more readily, and even read difficulty differently, when an action fits a currently salient identity \citep{oyserman2009}.}

Simulation evidence supports this claim (Figure~\ref{fig:framing_dynamics}d, e). Action transition matrices show a substantial RECALL$\to$ENGAGE path in healthy agents. Post-action valence stability analysis reveals that RECALL produces lower 5-step valence variance than FEEL or FUTURATE, confirming that RECALL \emph{stabilises} affect rather than merely boosting it. Stabilisation is the computational basis for the claim: by anchoring beliefs in the recalled valence target (positive past in healthy agents via the bidirectional $\mathbf{B}_v^{\textsc{recall}}$ pull), RECALL reduces the volatility of subsequent prediction errors, creating a more stable platform for engagement.

The positivity parameter should be read as positive \emph{recall}: the capacity to sample autobiographical material that remains affectively usable by the current self-model. When recent evidence raises VFE, the agent may fail to generate precise positive recall because current affective and interoceptive state constrains access to specific autobiographical material. A decline in positivity therefore signals that relying on past beliefs is becoming less useful, and shifts policy selection toward explicit future-oriented planning despite its higher energy cost.

\subsection{When Past Framing Fails: Feedback Reliance}\label{sec:feedback_reliance}

This expectation of positive core beliefs is ultimately constrained by environmental feedback. While cue-triggered retrieval is efficient for cost-effective appetitive policy selection, it is also highly sensitive to retrieval bias. Fluctuations in positive beliefs under recent VFE dynamics thus parallel key features of affective bias in memory retrieval, and their consequences for state-to-state planning resolution.

When $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ is low, RECALL fails at two distinct levels. First, the \emph{mechanism itself is impaired}: $\alpha \approx 0.14$, so $\mathbf{B}_v^{\textsc{recall}}$ barely pulls valence toward the prior; the agent's generative model can no longer generate effective positive counterfactuals from past experience. In the generative process, recall effectiveness is independently gated by the same biological parameter ($\sigma(\pi_{\text{pos}} - \theta) \approx 0.14$), so even when RECALL is selected, it succeeds only 14\% of the time. Second, \emph{deployment is suppressed}: the weakened mechanism makes RECALL EFE-uncompetitive, as its predicted valence gain no longer justifies its energy cost. The agent stops selecting RECALL not because it ``decides'' recall is unhelpful, but because EFE minimisation routes away from a policy whose transition dynamics can no longer reduce risk. These two failures are coupled but conceptually distinct: the first is a failure of allostatic support (the generative model can no longer sustain positive self-beliefs), while the second is an emergent consequence of the first within the EFE framework.

{\rr Interpreted through \S\ref{sec:retrieval_constraints}, this is the computational form of \emph{overgeneral memory}: when the self-model cannot sustain a coherent positive self, the descent of the autobiographical hierarchy can no longer reach a self-congruent specific episode and truncates. The cue is answered, \footnote{in the language of the CaR-FA-X programme, from the kind of person one takes oneself to be, at the categorical--semantic level, rather than from an episode that would have to be reconciled with the self-concept \citep{williamsetal2007, watkinsteasdale2001}} => this is the loss of precision with positivity. The compensatory reallocation observed below is the predicted consequence of the one-regulator account of \S\ref{sec:one_regulator}: deprived of the cheap optimism heuristic, the agent must ``zoom in'' and pay for resolution elsewhere, and where it pays reveals which substitute pathways remain open.}

Figure~\ref{fig:feedback_reliance} demonstrates this pattern by comparing two profiles with identical reward sensitivity ($c_{\text{scale}} = 1.0$), granularity ($K = 8$), and interoceptive precision ($\omega_e = 5.0$), differing only in positive-belief precision: healthy ($\pi_{\text{pos}} = 5.0$) versus recall-impaired ($\pi_{\text{pos}} = 0.2$). The recall-impaired agent shows a near-complete loss of RECALL utilisation (from 27\% to $<$1\%), with the freed probability mass reallocating to ABSTRACT (34\%), FEEL (30\%), ENGAGE (18\%), and notably FUTURATE (15\%). The compensatory pattern is telling: the agent replaces backward-looking narrative stabilisation with ungrounded future-oriented cognition (ABSTRACT), interoceptive self-monitoring (FEEL), environmental engagement (ENGAGE), and direct prospective simulation (FUTURATE). {\rr The substitution of ABSTRACT and FUTURATE for RECALL is the model's version of the observation that if when self-anchored retrieval is unavailable but construal pressure persists, cognition migrates to representations that are abstract without being self-congruent. When a habit prior is added (biasing the agent toward RECALL despite its ineffectiveness), the compensatory pathway is blocked and the agent becomes locked into past-directed processing, a qualitatively different pathology from recall impairment alone. \section{Future-Oriented Framing: Reactive Planning}\label{sec:future}

\subsection{Mechanism of FUTURATE}\label{sec:futurate_mechanism}

The FUTURATE policy redistributes precision toward anticipated future states. $\mathbf{B}_v^{\textsc{futurate}}$ pulls valence toward the solution state $K{-}1$ (50\% pull strength), modelling the generation of a highly precise prospective counterfactual. The energy cost is moderate ($-0.5$ per step), reflecting the metabolic expense of prospective simulation. $\mathbf{B}_f^{\textsc{futurate}}$ is 90\% absorbing: once the agent enters future-oriented framing, narrative inertia keeps it there.

In FUTURATE, the agent lays out candidate action--outcome sequences and scores them against preferred states \citep{leiboetal2024}. The policy turns a coarse intention into more resolved steps, paying energy for a level of detail that RECALL does not require \citep{pezzulocisek2016, shenhavbotvinickcohen2013}.

The EFE profile of FUTURATE is distinctive: it has the \emph{lowest} valence risk (because the $\mathbf{B}_v$ pull maximally aligns predicted observations with preferences) but the \emph{highest} energy risk (because of the metabolic cost). The tension between valence benefit and energy cost brakes FUTURATE selection.

As FUTURATE unrolls a plan in finer grain, positivity becomes less central than feasibility. Coarse, distant construals can remain optimistic because many implementation details are still unspecified \citep{tropeliberman2010, sharot2011}. Once the agent moves into fine-grained prospection, policy value depends more on predicted state transitions and energy cost than on the desirability of autobiographical material.

\subsection{Triggered by High Prediction Error}\label{sec:futurate_trigger}

When the agent's beliefs are far from preferred (high VFE), all policies have large risk terms. However, FUTURATE has the largest risk \emph{reduction} because $\mathbf{B}_v^{\textsc{futurate}}$ pulls most strongly toward the preferred state. The standard softmax $\pi(a) \propto \exp(-\gamma \cdot G(a))$ selects FUTURATE when VFE is high as a consequence of the EFE decomposition. A spike in prediction error is the case where forward planning can outweigh the cheaper self-anchored policy \citep{schwabewolf2009, ottoraio2013}. No separate arbitration between controllers is needed: prediction error makes the coarse plan uncompetitive within the same generative model.

Simulation evidence confirms this (Figure~\ref{fig:framing_dynamics}b, f). Mean VFE conditioned on selected action shows that FUTURATE is preferentially selected at higher-VFE timesteps than RECALL or ENGAGE. The VFE--$\pi(\text{FUTURATE})$ correlation is monotonically positive in healthy and high-sensitivity agents, and flat when hedonic sensitivity is asymmetric (consistent with blunted positive reward rendering all policies similarly valued).

{\rr Moreover, we interpret the pull exerted by RECALL as depending not on positive beliefs per se, but on positive \emph{recall}: the agent's capacity to sample autobiographical material into narrative structures that sustain agency, growth, continuity, and self-coherence. This sampling is strongly influenced by recent valenced feedback, interoceptive precision, emotional granularity, and the current affective state of the agent, the retrieval constraints of \S\ref{sec:retrieval_constraints}. The relevant issue is therefore not simply whether the agent possesses positive or negative beliefs, but whether autobiographical material can be assimilated into a coherent self-narrative. This is particularly important in the case of adverse experience: agents exposed to aversive events may not merely possess negative memories; they may lack precise, narratively integrated autobiographical representations capable of supporting adaptive self-projection. This corresponds to the overgeneral autobiographical memory of \S\S\ref{sec:retrieval_constraints} and \ref{sec:feedback_reliance}, in which recall becomes diffuse, categorical, and insufficiently anchored in specific episodes \citep{williamsetal2007, watkinsteasdale2001}, and it explains why the same agents who cannot stabilise on the past are the ones driven, by default, into unresolved forward projection.}

\subsection{Shorter Behavioural Horizons}\label{sec:short_horizon}

Step-by-step planning is costly, so the model prices it directly in the EFE term \citep{dawnivdayan2005, koolcushmangershman2016, shenhavbotvinickcohen2013, keramatismittenaar2016}. FUTURATE's energy cost creates a natural brake. After one to two steps of FUTURATE, the predicted interoceptive observation enters the ``depleted'' range, which increases the energy-modality risk term. The softmax shifts away from FUTURATE, producing short reactive planning bursts rather than sustained commitment.

Simulation evidence (Figure~\ref{fig:framing_dynamics}c) confirms this brake. The healthy agent selects FUTURATE at a low but non-zero rate ($\approx 6\%$), producing short reactive planning bursts that are quickly terminated by the energy cost. RECALL, FEEL, and ENGAGE cycle with short run lengths ($\approx 1.6$, $1.3$, and $1.2$ timesteps, respectively), and FUTURATE bursts are similarly brief ($\approx 1.2$ steps). When reward sensitivity is strongly amplified ($c_{\text{scale}} = 6.0$), the energy brake is overridden, producing FUTURATE run lengths of $\approx 2.7$ timesteps, the longest of any action, with 63\% selection rate. The contrast is mechanistically transparent: RECALL has minimal energy cost and FEEL actively restores energy, while FUTURATE's energy cost provides an inherent self-limiting mechanism that is overcome only when reward amplification makes the valence risk reduction large enough to outweigh the energy penalty. {\rr This override is the mechanistic precursor of the pathological fixation developed below: what fails in pathology is not the recruitment of the deep mode but the pricing signal that normally returns the regulator to shallow.}

\subsection{Back to Narrative: Re-ascent of the Depth Regulator}\label{sec:back_to_narrative}

{\rr Once the effortful burst has done its work, the healthy agent zooms back out, re-integrating what it found into the ongoing narrative. This closes the loop anticipated in \S\ref{sec:one_regulator}: coarse, optimism-heuristic framing and fine-grained effortful planning \emph{alternate} rather than either dominating, because a single regulator sets the depth and both directions of movement are EFE-motivated, descent by prediction error, re-ascent by energy cost and restored prior precision. The cycle can be read as the sequence:}

\[
\resizebox{\linewidth}{!}{$
\text{self-anchored affective stabilisation}
\longrightarrow
\text{exploratory present engagement}
\longrightarrow
\text{new experience}
\longrightarrow
\text{episodic encoding}
\longrightarrow
\text{updated narrative reliance}
$}
\]

{\rr Each new experience, episodically encoded, feeds fresh \emph{correspondence} back into \emph{self-coherence} and renews the platform for RECALL-supported engagement \citep{conwaysingertagini2004, schacteraddisbuckner2007}. Note also that the re-ascent is not merely a matter of saving computation: as \S\ref{sec:optimism} argued against the pure cost--benefit reading, a familiar routine loses its appeal once it stops being informative, and boredom is the aversive signal of that loss \citep{stephanetal2016, schwartenbecketal2019}. The healthy cycle is driven as much by the pull of uncertainty resolution, new experience to encode, as by the push of effort avoidance.}

\subsection{Frame Beliefs and Temporal Orientation}\label{sec:frame_beliefs}

The posterior frame belief $q(f \mid o)$, obtained by marginalising the full posterior over valence and energy, provides a direct readout of the agent's believed temporal orientation for policy selection. Healthy agents show frame allocation dominated by present ($\approx 0.47$) with balanced past and future ($\approx 0.22$ and $\approx 0.32$), reflecting the adaptive RECALL--FEEL--ENGAGE--FUTURATE cycle (Figure~\ref{fig:framing_dynamics}a). Agents with a strong habit prior toward RECALL show past-dominated frame belief ($\approx 0.36$), while agents with amplified reward sensitivity and impaired interoception show dominant future-frame belief ($\approx 0.68$) from FUTURATE dominance. These parameter-driven frame profiles correspond to distinct temporal fixation patterns that a companion paper develops as clinical phenotypes.

\subsection{Self-Regulation Versus Self-Amplification}\label{sec:self_reg}

The emotion--action--inference loop (\S\ref{sec:loop}) admits two dynamical regimes. Under \emph{self-regulation}, the loop is homeostatic: high VFE triggers corrective action selection, which reduces prediction error; positive $v_{\text{action}}$ reinforces precision on the corrective trajectory \citep{joffily2013emotional}. In the healthy default, prediction errors are informative signals that improve the model. {\rr Cognitive control is deployed adaptively: the agent sets the resolution of its own deliberation, and moves it, rather than committing to a fixed horizon, which is the graded, single-regulator picture of \S\ref{sec:one_regulator} rather than an arbitration between two controllers.}

Under \emph{self-amplification}, the loop becomes pathological. When temporal framing locks into a single mode, precision on that mode's prediction errors increases, and the agent cannot update away from the locked frame. {\rr This is where the double bind that closes \S\ref{sec:one_regulator} is realised: because adaptive economy and pathological truncation are two settings of one state-dependent mechanism, sustained stress does not merely \emph{recruit} the effortful branch, it degrades the agent's ability to modulate its depth at all.} Chronic stress represents the self-amplifying pathology of temporal framing.


\section{Chronic Stress as Maladaptive Temporal Fixation}\label{sec:chronic_stress}

\subsection{The Pathological Cycle}\label{sec:pathological_cycle}

{\rr Chronic stress is the computational realisation of the \emph{double bind} identified at the close of \S\ref{sec:one_regulator}: when affect turns negative but construal stays coarse, the agent is left with neither pathway to policy selection. The shallow route cannot deliver, the optimism heuristic is available only while affect is positive enough to sustain a coherent, self-congruent prior, hence a functioning RECALL with high $\pi_{\text{pos}}$. The deep route delivers only frustrated, non-resolving projection, precise plans that a volatile world keeps falsifying. What should have been a brief, energy-braked FUTURATE burst becomes an unbraked fixation. The two temporal directions of this failure recover the rumination--worry split of \S\ref{sec:one_regulator} \citep{fresco2002, nolenhoeksema2008}: rumination, past-directed and self-focused, when a habitual bias overrides the escape that consequence-based control would favour, the identity heuristic run compulsively over a self whose coherence pressure now conserves only negative generalisations (\S\ref{sec:retrieval_constraints}); worry, future-directed and anticipatory, when a bias toward reward overrides the cost that normally limits forward simulation, coupled with poor interoceptive processing.}


We introduce a ``stressed'' profile characterised by four concurrent perturbations from the healthy baseline: very high environmental volatility ($0.9$ vs $0.3$), elevated reward sensitivity ($c_{\text{scale}} = 2.0$ vs $1.0$), stress-impaired interoception ($\omega_e = 0.5$ vs $5.0$), and reduced positive-belief precision ($\pi_{\text{pos}} = 2.5$ vs $5.0$). Chronic stress is associated with reduced interoceptive accuracy \citep{barrett2017theory, seth2016interoceptive}. Since interoception constrains access to specific autobiographical material (\S\ref{sec:retrieval_constraints}), degrading it should disturb the depth regulator itself, not merely one policy.

These perturbations interact through the EFE decomposition in three reinforcing ways. First, impaired interoception ($\omega_e = 0.5$) blurs the agent's $\mathbf{A}_{\text{int}}$ matrix, reducing the energy-modality risk term that would normally deter FUTURATE selection and simultaneously eliminating the interoceptive differentiation that supports FEEL; in effect, both the brake and the body-awareness pathway are disabled. Second, elevated reward sensitivity ($c_{\text{scale}} = 2.0$) amplifies the hedonic preference vectors $\mathbf{C}_{\text{ext}}$ and $\mathbf{C}_{\text{val}}$, increasing the valence-modality risk reduction for FUTURATE; the accelerator is enhanced. Third, under the standard softmax policy selection $\pi(a) \propto \exp(-\gamma \cdot G(a))$, FUTURATE and RECALL enter the policy mix once the brake is disabled and the accelerator is enhanced, producing the rumination-plus-worry pattern. {\rr This makes formal the mechanism anticipated in \S\ref{sec:one_regulator}: stress taxes the very machinery that would deliver controlled planning. It is the loss of the \emph{brake}, not the loss of effort, that converts adaptive reactive planning into fixation; the stressed agent is, if anything, over-deliberating, at a resolution it can no longer afford and to no resolving effect.}

Once FUTURATE enters the policy mix, its 90\% absorbing frame transition creates narrative inertia, causing the agent to persist in future-oriented framing. Meanwhile, RECALL's strong past-frame transition ($\approx 70\%$) creates a complementary backward pull. The volatile environment keeps violating the agent's precise predictions from both directions, producing chronic positive $\Delta F$ (increasing VFE). DISSOCIATE fills the gaps between directed temporal processing, its diffuse frame transition spreading temporal belief across all horizons. {\rr DISSOCIATE, on the one-regulator reading, is the state in which neither setting is occupied: not the shallow, self-anchored mode, which requires a coherent prior, nor the deep, task-anchored mode, which requires an affordable rollout, but a disengagement of the regulator itself, temporal unmooring.} The result is a self-amplifying cycle: directed temporal processing (RECALL + FUTURATE) produces precise predictions, which the environment violates, producing negative $v_{\text{model}}$, which elevates VFE and further favours these over present-oriented engagement (\S\ref{sec:futurate_trigger}).

\subsection{Temporal Dysregulation Pattern}\label{sec:temporal_dysreg}

The stressed agent's posterior frame belief shows a characteristic pattern of future-dominated temporal fixation: dominant \textsc{future} ($0.72$ vs $0.30$ in healthy), with severely depleted \textsc{present} ($0.19$ vs $0.48$) and reduced \textsc{past} ($0.09$ vs $0.22$). The stressed agent is drawn overwhelmingly toward future framing at the expense of present engagement (Figure~\ref{fig:chronic_stress}a). The action profile confirms this: FUTURATE dominates (71\%), supplemented by DISSOCIATE (20\%) and ABSTRACT (3\%), while present-oriented ENGAGE collapses (4\%) and both RECALL and FEEL disappear ($\approx$1\%).

This pattern of dominant future framing with depleted present engagement is the computational signature of anxious prospective simulation, the ``worry'' component of chronic stress \citep{berg2022rumination, fresco2002}. 
FUTURATE dominance reflects the interaction of reward hypersensitivity ($c_{\text{scale}} = 2.0$) with impaired interoception ($\omega_e = 0.5$) under very high volatility (0.9): the energy brake is disabled (the agent cannot predict depletion) while the hedonic accelerator is enhanced (amplified $\mathbf{C}_{\text{val}}$ increases FUTURATE's risk reduction). The near-absence of RECALL ($\approx 1\%$) reflects the reduced $\pi_{\text{pos}} = 2.5$ ($\alpha \approx 0.62$, recall target at $\approx 57\%$ of max valence), which makes RECALL EFE-uncompetitive against the strong FUTURATE pull. {\rr This is the other jaw of the double bind: the agent can neither stabilise on a positive past, nor resolve the future, the consequence heuristic never lands.} The presence of DISSOCIATE (20\%) reflects periods of temporal unmooring (disengagement from directed processing), consistent with dissociative episodes reported under chronic stress. FEEL is entirely absent: the stressed agent's impaired interoception renders interoceptive processing uninformative, blocking the body-awareness pathway that healthy agents use for self-regulation.

It is the forward pole of the double bind occupied on its own. The near-absence of RECALL ($\approx 1\%$) shows that the backward pole, rumination, is \emph{not} co-engaged here: the reduced $\pi_{\text{pos}} = 2.5$ ($\alpha \approx 0.62$, recall target at $\approx 57\%$ of max valence) leaves RECALL without a positive landing and hence EFE-uncompetitive against the strong FUTURATE pull, so the agent tips forward rather than backward. Rumination would enter only under a habit prior that overrode this EFE gradient.

\subsection{Persistent Backward Negative Valence}\label{sec:backward_negative}

The stressed agent's backward valence $v_{\text{model}} = \tanh(-\Delta F / \tau)$ is persistently more negative than the healthy agent's (Figure~\ref{fig:chronic_stress}b). The mechanism is precise: the volatile environment keeps violating the agent's predictions (producing positive $\Delta F$), so the backward channel, which evaluates how well past predictions matched observations, is persistently negative.

FUTURATE exacerbates this effect. By pulling beliefs toward the solution state, FUTURATE creates \emph{precise predictions about positive future outcomes} that the volatile environment then violates, amplifying $\Delta F$. {\rr This is the computational face of the point made in \S\ref{sec:optimism}: coarse long-horizon optimism is computationally sound precisely because it commits to a direction rather than a trajectory, whereas spelling out distant detail tends to produce anxiety rather than mastery \citep{sharot2011, fristonetal2021sophisticated}. Make the optimistic future \emph{precise}, and a volatile world will falsify it step by step \citep{tropeliberman2010}. The stressed agent has, in effect, taken the deep-planning grain of the dangerous-city traveller and applied it to the desirability content of the safe-city plan, inheriting the costs of both and the protections of neither.} The backward negative valence is therefore not a belief about the past being bad, but a \emph{signal} that the model's fit to recent observations is deteriorating, a signal amplified by the very mechanism (FUTURATE) that the agent uses to cope.

\subsection{Channel Decomposition}\label{sec:channel_decomp}

The stressed agent's valence channel decomposition reveals the full temporal structure of chronic stress (Figure~\ref{fig:chronic_stress}c). The reward channel ($v_{\text{reward}} = -0.12$) is the dominant driver of negative valence: FUTURATE's $\mathbf{B}_v$ pull (50\% toward max valence) elevates expected utility $\mathbb{E}[U]$, but the highly volatile environment delivers observations below expectation ($U < \mathbb{E}[U]$), producing persistent negative hedonic surprise. The backward channel ($v_{\text{model}} = +0.01$) is only mildly positive, as the high volatility produces roughly symmetric prediction errors. The action channel ($v_{\text{action}} = +0.06$) remains positive: the agent keeps finding ``better'' policies under high VFE (policy improvement is easy when the current policy is poor), but the improvement is ephemeral because the environment keeps changing.

Strongly negative reward valence alongside persistent forward positivity is the computational signature of \emph{frustrated forward projection}: the agent is trapped in optimistic planning that systematically fails to deliver, producing the chronic hedonic disappointment characteristic of stressed overmentalising. {\rr The channel decomposition also shows why this is \emph{worry} and not rumination. Worry is forward-dominant: its signature is persistent positive $v_{\text{action}}$ (FUTURATE keeps finding ``better'' policies) alongside negative $v_{\text{reward}}$ (the world underdelivers). Rumination would show the mirror signature, backward-dominant, carried by persistently negative $v_{\text{model}}$ from a RECALL locked on a self it cannot render positive. The two are the same failure of unresolving repetitive thought \citep{nolenhoeksema2008, watkinsteasdale2001, ehring2008repetitive} read at opposite ends of the temporal-orientation axis of \S\ref{sec:temporal_orientation}.}

\section{A temporal cartography of emotions}


\subsection{The VFE/EFE Temporal Mapping}\label{sec:temporal_mapping}


These shifts in temporal orientation are intertwined with specific emotions: regret is directed towards the past, surprise refocuses attention on the present, and hope is about the future.
We propose that emotions inherit their temporal direction from whichever free energy quantity generates them. This yields a systematic taxonomy. 
={\rr This taxonomy of emotions should be understood as a possible conceptual landscape mapped across distinct trajectories of free-energy dynamics, rather than an essentialist or universal classification. The actual partitioning of this space within a given agent, and their subsequent ability to navigate it, plausibly depends on how emotional concepts were carved out during their own developmental history, sociocultural contexts, and the unique functional demands of the agent's ecological niche. 

As such, as in section XX, we follow Barrett in understanding emotions as constructing concepts, meaning that individuals must be in possession of such learned concepts in order to experience the emotion of, say, guilt or shame, as opposed to just valenced affect

Benedict’s essentialist characterization of “guilt" versus "shame" cultures \citep{benedict1946chrysanthemum} has subsequently been reframed in terms of culturally acquired self-construals, as in Markus and Kitayama’s distinction between independent and interdependent selves \citep{markus1991culture}, or in terms of the demands of the social niche, with Sznycer et al. emphasizing the role of high versus low relational mobility across societies \citep{sznycer2013cross}. As such, the distribution of shame and guilt among individuals raised in distinct ecological niches may differ, and so do the higher more conceptual layers of their generative models. Yet each, shame and guilt are associated with distinct rumination versus problem solving modes; we see how our model interprets it.
}

\paragraph{Backward (VFE-generated).} Emotions grounded in the evaluation of past model fit include \emph{regret} (VFE higher than counterfactual alternative), \emph{guilt} (VFE elevated due to own action), \emph{shame} (VFE elevated due to social model violation), \emph{pride} (VFE lower than expected), and \emph{gratitude} (VFE reduction attributed to another agent).

\paragraph{Present ($-dF/dt$ or RPE).} Emotions grounded in the rate of change at the current moment include \emph{joy} (rapid VFE decrease), \emph{anger} (VFE increase attributed to external agent), \emph{disgust} (VFE increase in interoceptive modality), and \emph{surprise} (large absolute $\Delta F$).

\paragraph{Forward (EFE-generated).} Emotions grounded in the evaluation of expected future performance include \emph{hope} (low EFE under preferred policy), \emph{fear} (high EFE across all policies), \emph{anxiety} (high EFE with high ambiguity), and \emph{anticipation} (low EFE with low ambiguity).

\paragraph{Transitional (VFE vs prior EFE).} Emotions that arise from comparing current VFE with previously computed EFE include \emph{relief} (VFE lower than EFE had predicted), \emph{disappointment} (VFE higher than EFE had predicted), and \emph{satisfaction} (VFE confirms low EFE prediction).

The mapping extends the appraisal-theoretic classification of \citet{smith1985patterns} and the OCC model of \citet{ortony1988cognitive} by grounding the temporal dimension in the formal distinction between variational and expected free energy, rather than in folk-psychological temporal labels.





\subsection{Counterfactual Emotions and Temporal Simulation}\label{sec:counterfactual_emotions}

One class of emotions requires maintaining beliefs about unchosen policies. \citet{hesp2020sophisticated} showed that \emph{sophisticated} affective inference involves evaluating counterfactual trajectories: the agent computes what \emph{would have} happened under alternative policies, and the divergence between actual and counterfactual VFE generates emotions like regret and relief.

Formally, regret at time $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Regret} = F_{\text{actual}}(t) - \min_{\pi'} F_{\text{counterfactual}}(t \mid \pi')
\end{equation}
Computing $F_{\text{counterfactual}}$ requires running the generative model \emph{offline}, simulating what would have happened under the unchosen policy. That is what temporal framing does: the agent simulates past states (RECALL) or future states (FUTURATE) by running its generative model in a mode decoupled from current sensory input. Counterfactual emotions thus require the same computational machinery as temporal framing. Temporal framing may be the general mechanism, and counterfactual emotions its specific affective products.

In the simulations, this offline operation is implemented as a short counterfactual rollout. For each candidate first action, the agent predicts the latent state reached by that action, recursively evaluates subsequent policies from that imagined state, and selects from the resulting rollout EFE. We also store the diagnostic quantity $G(a_{\text{chosen}}) - \min_a G(a)$, which records how far the selected action sits from the best available road not taken. This remains a bounded approximation: it rolls over latent beliefs rather than sampling a full observation-conditioned policy tree.



\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Temporal Aiming as a Unified Theory}\label{sec:unified_theory}

The VFE/EFE distinction provides a temporal taxonomy of emotion. This has been implicit in the active inference literature but, until now, never stated in a single account. Variational free energy concerns beliefs about what \emph{has happened} and generates backward-oriented affect (regret, guilt, pride, gratitude). Expected free energy concerns expectations about what \emph{will happen} and generates forward-oriented affect (hope, fear, anxiety, anticipation). The rate of change at the current moment generates present-oriented affect (joy, anger, surprise). Transitional emotions (relief, disappointment) arise from comparing current VFE with prior EFE predictions.

Counterfactual temporal framing is the \emph{mechanism} by which agents actively navigate this temporal structure. Where \citet{hinrichs2025temporal} addressed temporal aiming in dyadic geometric hyperscanning, we have addressed the single-agent computational mechanism: temporal framing as precision redistribution across temporal horizons, selected by EFE minimisation.

{\rr 
\subsection{The role of affect and emotions in attention distribution}
This structure allows us to distinguish between a thin and a thick interpretation of affect. On the thin interpretation, affect signals the recent or expected trajectories of free energy in the generative process, informing a subsequent step of temporal framing. On the thick interpretation, affect constitutes the very enactive force that motivates and directs attentional allocation across different temporal horizons. For example, [...]. This leter view extends Kiverstein's (2022) account of affect, as a necessary felt incentive through which action becomes possible.}
The more precise cartography of emotions across these free-energy regularities, the more precise allocation of attention across temporal orientations (see Section 12.2 for a discussion of the role of these emotions). The framework therefore offers a possible mechanism by which the capacity to experience a wide and finely differentiated range of emotions is itself adaptive. 


\subsection{Temporal Framing as a General Mechanism}\label{sec:general_mechanism}

Temporal framing is not specific to pathology. Healthy agents \emph{use} all six temporal modes for adaptive regulation. RECALL stabilises affect (creating a floor under valence), FEEL manages the energy budget through interoceptive processing, ENGAGE maintains environmental coupling, FUTURATE provides reactive planning bursts ($\approx 7\%$ of timesteps, responding to high prediction error), ABSTRACT provides a secondary hedonic pathway, and DISSOCIATE provides brief temporal unmooring during transitions. The moderate energy cost of FUTURATE ($-0.5$) provides a self-limiting brake that keeps it adaptive rather than dominant. Pathology arises not from temporal framing itself but from the failure of its balancing mechanisms: when the brake is disabled (impaired interoception) and the accelerator is strongly amplified, FUTURATE runs unchecked; when the accelerator is enhanced and the environment is volatile, forward projection dominates with frustrated reward; when hedonic sensitivity is asymmetric and a habit prior locks the agent onto RECALL, the temporal repertoire constricts onto past-directed processing.

{\rr
However it remains inherently bounded because it does not explicitly evaluate how future observations will update beliefs and reshape the model used to interpret subsequent states. It is simply selecting the path most likely to lead to sensory outcomes that match existing beliefs. This limitation has been addressed in recent developments in sophisticated active inference extend this future-oriented architecture into an explicitly metacognitive framework by replacing open-loop policy selection with recursive tree searches over future belief states (Friston et al., 2021). In sophisticated architecture there is the recursive evaluating future belief updates alongside future state transitions (Parr et al., 2022). Rather than considering only the environmental consequences of an action, the agent also evaluates how those consequences are expected to transform its own model of the world. Future trajectories are therefore assessed in terms of both the states they produce and the beliefs they are expected to induce, supporting more adaptive regulation of uncertainty and behaviour over time (Stephan et al., 2016).

}
\subsection{Metacognitive approach}

\subsection{Metastability as Health}\label{sec:metastability}

The simulations suggest that health is not a particular temporal configuration but the \emph{capacity to transition between configurations}. What defines the healthy agent is not its specific action mix (FEEL~29\%, RECALL~23\%, ENGAGE~23\%, ABSTRACT~13\%, FUTURATE~7\%, DISSOCIATE~3\%) but its high switching rate and short mean run length. Five distinct temporal modes are actively deployed, none dominating for more than a few consecutive steps. In dynamical systems terms, this is metastability: the agent visits multiple attractor basins without becoming trapped in any one.

Pathology, on this account, is \emph{stickiness}. When reward sensitivity is strongly amplified and interoception is impaired, FUTURATE dominates (mean $\approx 2.7$ steps, 64\% selection rate), reflecting persistent lock-in to forward projection via absorptive narrative inertia. When a habit prior biases the agent toward RECALL, mode constriction takes a different form: the habit prior locks the agent into past-directed processing while suppressing the escape routes that EFE would favour. Both pathologies reduce the agent's effective temporal repertoire, but through different mechanisms: the first through the interaction of frame self-transitions with amplified reward, the second through habitual override of EFE-optimal policy selection.

An agent locked into ENGAGE during a crisis is not pathological; context-appropriate dominance of a single mode is adaptive. The pathology lies in the inability to \emph{exit} that mode when context changes. Habit priors make temporal framing ``sticky'' in a way that EFE minimisation alone cannot correct: the agent is trapped not by the EFE landscape but by the habitual override of it. Restoring the ability to transition between temporal orientations matters more than correcting the balance between them.

\subsection{Connection to Attention-as-Precision}\label{sec:attention_connection}

\citet{feldman2010attention} established that attention is precision weighting on prediction errors. Our framework extends this to the temporal domain: \emph{temporal attention} is precision weighting on temporal horizons. The $\mathbf{B}_{\text{frame}}$ matrices function as temporal attention operators, and action selection in the temporal framing domain amounts to deciding \emph{where in time} to attend. This connects temporal framing to the predictive processing literature on selective attention. Spatial attention (``where to look'') and temporal attention (``when to think about'') may be instances of the same precision-weighting mechanism applied to different dimensions of the generative model.

\subsection{Mood Versus Emotion Timescales}\label{sec:mood_emotion}

\citet{hesp2021deeply} distinguished two timescales in the affect hierarchy: M4 (emotion), operating within-trial as fast precision dynamics, and M5 (mood), operating cross-trial as slow valence state inference. Temporal framing operates at the \emph{interface} between these timescales. Individual frame selections are M4 phenomena, reflecting fast, within-trial decisions about temporal orientation driven by current EFE. Sustained frame biases are M5 phenomena, reflecting slow, cross-trial tendencies toward particular temporal orientations that encode learned or dispositional patterns.

We implement the M5 layer as a \emph{hierarchical POMDP} over the positive-belief precision parameter $\pi_{\text{pos}}$. The mood level maintains a categorical belief $q(\mu)$ over $N_\mu = 8$ discretised mood states $\mu \in \{0.5, 1.5, \ldots, 7.5\}$, each corresponding to a value of $\pi_{\text{pos}}$. Every $T_\mu = 50$ emotion-level steps, the mood level performs one cycle of Bayesian inference:

\textbf{Observation.} The mood-level observation is the mean variational free energy (VFE) over the preceding window: $\bar{F} = T_\mu^{-1} \sum_{t' \in \text{window}} F(t')$. This is binned into $N_o^\mu = 5$ categories. The observation model $\mathbf{A}^\mu$ encodes the expectation that higher mood states (higher $\pi_{\text{pos}}$, hence better RECALL effectiveness) produce lower VFE:
\begin{equation}
    P(o^\mu \mid \mu) \propto \exp\!\left( -\frac{(\bar{o}^\mu - \hat{F}(\mu))^2}{2\sigma_A^2} \right), \qquad \hat{F}(\mu) = 4.14 + 0.66\,(1 - \sigma(\mu - 2))
\end{equation}
where $\hat{F}(\mu)$ is the expected VFE given mood state $\mu$ and $\sigma_A = 0.2$.

\textbf{Transition.} The mood-level transition model $\mathbf{B}^\mu$ is near-diagonal (self-transition probability $0.97$, symmetric $\pm 1$ transitions at $0.015$), encoding the empirical observation that mood is highly persistent. All asymmetry in mood drift arises from the VFE evidence, not from built-in bias.

\textbf{Update.} Standard Bayesian filtering:
\begin{align}
    q_{\text{pred}}(\mu) &= \mathbf{B}^\mu\, q(\mu) \\
    q(\mu \mid o^\mu) &\propto P(o^\mu \mid \mu)\, q_{\text{pred}}(\mu)
\end{align}

\textbf{Readout.} The expected $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ under the mood posterior parameterises the emotion-level $\mathbf{B}$ matrices:
\begin{equation}
    \pi_{\text{pos}} = \mathbb{E}_{q(\mu)}[\mu] = \sum_i \mu_i\, q(\mu_i)
\end{equation}

The hierarchical architecture creates self-reinforcing attractor dynamics from Bayesian inference alone. A healthy agent in a benign environment experiences low VFE, which is consistent with high mood states, maintaining $\pi_{\text{pos}}$ and RECALL effectiveness. Conversely, chronic prediction error (high VFE) shifts the mood posterior toward lower states, weakening RECALL, which further elevates VFE, producing a low-mood attractor. Affective profiles thus emerge as stable attractors of the hierarchical mood dynamics rather than being imposed as fixed parameter settings.

The transition from adaptive temporal framing (M4) to chronic temporal fixation (M5) is precisely the self-amplification pathology described in \S\ref{sec:chronic_stress}. We demonstrate this with a stress decay experiment (Figure~\ref{fig:stress_decay_p2}): two agents with identical healthy initial conditions ($\pi_{\text{pos}} = 5.0$) are placed in environments differing only in volatility ($0.3$ vs $0.9$). Over $T = 3000$ steps, the hierarchical mood posterior diverges dramatically: the healthy agent's $\mathbb{E}[\pi_{\text{pos}}]$ drifts upward toward $\approx 7.5$ (positive attractor), while the stressed agent's posterior crashes downward through the sigmoid knee at $\pi_{\text{pos}} = 2$, settling near $\approx 0.8$, well below the threshold where RECALL effectiveness collapses entirely (panel~c). The divergence arises from accumulated VFE evidence at the mood level alone, without any change to the agent's initial parameter configuration.

\subsection{Clinical Relevance}\label{sec:clinical}

The temporal framing framework extends to clinical applications. Different parameter configurations produce qualitatively distinct temporal fixation patterns: asymmetric hedonic sensitivity combined with a habit prior produces past-directed lock-in, amplified reward sensitivity with impaired interoception produces future-directed lock-in, and their interaction gives rise to oscillatory dynamics. The three-channel valence architecture produces a principled ordering of composite valence across profiles: the healthy agent shows the highest mean valence ($V = +0.13$), while chronic stress is the only profile with \emph{negative} mean valence ($-0.04$), driven by strongly negative $v_{\text{reward}}$ from frustrated forward projection, consistent with experience sampling data \citep{wichers2010momentary}. Near-zero composite valence in habit-prior-dominated profiles reflects \emph{channel cancellation} (opposing temporal signals) rather than channel collapse (uniform attenuation), providing testable predictions for ESM studies that separately assess backward, present, and forward components of affect. A companion paper applies this framework to bipolar disorder, developing the full clinical characterisation with granularity sweeps, parameter landscape analysis, valence--dominance trajectories, interoceptive load coupling, and psychotic decompensation.

\subsection{PAD Emotion Calibration}\label{sec:pad_validation}

To check that the generative model can span the affective space, we construct ten emotion profiles targeting distinct regions of the Pleasure--Arousal--Dominance (PAD) space \citep{mehrabian1996pleasure, russell1980circumplex}. Each profile is parameterised by a different combination of $K$, $\pi_{\text{pos}}$, $\omega_e$, $\gamma$, $c_{\text{scale}}$ (or the asymmetric $c_{\text{pos}}/c_{\text{neg}}$), the E~vector, and environmental volatility. PAD coordinates are not imposed during simulation. They are read out afterward from three-channel valence (pleasure), mean EFE magnitude (arousal), and policy entropy (dominance).

Table~\ref{tab:pad_profiles} summarises the results. The ten profiles cover all eight octants of the PAD cube, with qualitatively correct separation. Positive-valence profiles (happy, content, calm, excited) cluster in the $+P$ half-space; negative-valence profiles (angry, fearful, sad, depressed) cluster in the $-P$ half-space; and neutral profiles (alert, bored) sit near the $P \approx 0$ plane. The arousal dimension separates high-activation states (excited: $c_{\text{scale}} = 4.0$, $\gamma = 4$; angry: $c_{\text{scale}} = 5.0$) from low-activation states (calm: $c_{\text{scale}} = 0.7$; bored: $c_{\text{scale}} = 0.15$). The dominance dimension separates decisive agents (angry: $\gamma = 16$, D~$\approx 0.92$) from uncertain agents (excited: $\gamma = 4$, fearful: $\gamma = 4$).

\subsection{Empirical Validation Plan}\label{sec:empirical_validation}

The simulations make three claims that can be tested against existing data. First, the three-channel valence readout should recover within-person affect dynamics in experience-sampling data. We recovered the OSF emotion ESM dataset used in the empirical audit: 6,321 beep-level rows from 91 participants with repeated emotion-slider items. Positive and negative composites give a mean positive-minus-negative valence of $37.71$ (SD $29.80$), and repeated-item reliability is high ($r = .922$ across 6,321 paired observations). This dataset can benchmark measurement noise and within-person valence dynamics, although it does not contain direct temporal-orientation labels.

Second, asymmetric hedonic sensitivity should be fit against reward and punishment learning. The research pass identified OpenNeuro ds005356, a major-depressive-disorder/control MEG dataset with a probabilistic learning task. That dataset has not yet been downloaded or analysed in this workspace, so it should be treated as a target for the next empirical stage: fit $c_{\text{pos}}$ and $c_{\text{neg}}$ to trial-level choice data, then test whether the fitted asymmetry predicts clinical group and affective state.

Third, the RECALL mechanism should connect to autobiographical specificity. The recovered autobiographical-memory meta-analysis contains 181 effect sizes, including 117 Autobiographical Memory Test specificity effects. The mean Hedges $g$ for AMT specificity is $-0.747$, with similar negative effects for positive-specific cues ($g=-0.787$) and negative-specific cues ($g=-0.679$). These values provide group-level constraints on recall precision and specificity. They do not identify the full temporal-framing process on their own, but they anchor the claim that impaired positive recall is linked to less specific autobiographical retrieval.

The weakest empirical link remains temporal orientation itself. The current local audit identified ESM, reward-learning, and autobiographical-memory data, but not a verified open dataset that jointly measures affect, temporal orientation, and action selection at the same timescale. Until that dataset is located or collected, the model's frame-specific predictions should be presented as testable predictions rather than confirmed empirical results.

\subsection{Limitations and Future Directions}\label{sec:limitations}

The framework has several limitations. First, while the M5 mood layer implements a hierarchical POMDP over $\pi_{\text{pos}}$, the emotion-level policy selection remains single-step rather than multi-step planning \citep{friston2018deep}. Extending to deep temporal planning at the emotion level, with multi-step lookahead over temporal frame sequences, would better capture narrative entrenchment and flexibility.

Second, the temporal frame is modelled as a categorical variable ($\{$\textsc{past}, \textsc{present}, \textsc{future}$\}$) rather than a continuous temporal depth. In reality, ``the past'' admits degrees, from last week to childhood, and temporal framing may involve selecting a continuous temporal horizon rather than a discrete category.

Third, the E~vector (habit prior) is currently set as a fixed parameter rather than learned. In reality, ruminative habits develop over time through repeated activation \citep{watkins2008constructive}. A hierarchical model in which the E~vector is itself inferred from accumulated experience, analogous to the M5 mood layer's inference over $\pi_{\text{pos}}$, would capture the \emph{development} of habitual processing styles rather than assuming them as given.

Fourth, the model addresses single-agent temporal framing. Extending to dyadic and multi-agent settings, where agents' temporal frames interact and potentially synchronise, would connect to the geometric hyperscanning framework for affective co-regulation \citep{hinrichs2025temporal} and to work on how shared temporal orientation supports interpersonal coordination \citep{eldar2016mood}.
